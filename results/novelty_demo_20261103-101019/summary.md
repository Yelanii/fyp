# Novelty demonstration summary

- **C1 Real checkers repeat their false alarms:** SUPPORTED: after a false alarm the next check repeats it 76%-100% of the time, vs 0%-2% for a fresh false alarm. *Limit:* Two checkers from one family (Qwen2.5) on arithmetic steps.
- **C2 Expecting repeated false alarms wins with real checkers:** SUPPORTED (pre-registered R-E3a and R-E3b met): v4 -6.1% at T = 0, -5.5% at T = 0.7; lower in 16/16 conditions and on every seed. *Limit:* One planner and checker family (Qwen2.5), GSM8K tasks; tool prices and degradation are simulated; gain is mostly where wrong answers are costly (leak 3).
- **C3 The joint rule beats methods from the closest works:** SUPPORTED (pre-registered H-E6 confirmed): -13% vs VRR-Stop style; -17% vs ParetoBandit style; -17% vs Bench2Robust style. *Limit:* Simulation; the baselines are this project's adaptations of each paper's core idea (tuned on separate seeds), not their code.
- **C4 Checking pays most at moderate degradation:** SUPPORTED (pre-registered H-FA1 confirmed): peak +23.3% at 80% success vs -8.3% at 95% and +8.8% at 60% (5% false alarms). *Limit:* Simulation; the effect is strong when wrong answers are costly (3 tasks) and small when they cost 1 task.
- **C5 New method: learn the checker without labels (rule v5):** SUPPORTED: v5 -11.8% vs v4 without calibration; -1.0% vs v4 told the labels (H-V1 not confirmed); learned f within 0.028. *Limit:* Simulation, plus real checkers on simulated tool faults; cross-checking assumes a second tool whose output can be compared (part C tests comparisons that are sometimes wrong).
- **C6 Distance to the best possible policy:** SUPPORTED: rule v4 is +2.2% from the best possible policy and captures 92% of the achievable saving. *Limit:* Small instance only (two tools, single-step tasks, no retries); the Bayes policy is told the model.
- **C7 The rules hold on CostBench:** SUPPORTED: false-alarm-aware v4 beats the old rule v2 by 8.0% (p = 3.8e-06), and selective v5 beats cross-checking everything by 11.0% (p = 3.8e-06), 18 seeds. *Limit:* CostBench tasks and prices are real; the degradation and the checker are simulated. Never checking is only about 2% worse on CostBench, because a near-free route around the bad tool exists.
- **C8 Rule v5 beats v4 on CostBench:** NOT SUPPORTED: v5 vs v4 on CostBench +0.0% (p = 0.75). Avoiding the bad tool costs only about 0.1% more there, so checks rarely pay and learning the checker has little to improve. *Limit:* A tie, reported honestly: v5 helps when switching is costly (synthetic study, -11.8% vs v4 without labels), not when a cheap alternative exists.
- **C10 Rule v5 replaces labelled calibration on CostBench when checks are cheap:** SUPPORTED: with no labels, v5 is 5.2% below v4 without labels (p = 3.8e-06) and -0.3% from v4 that is given labels, 18 fresh seeds. *Limit:* The 5% condition was chosen on development seeds after C8 (declared in PREREGISTRATION_costbench_v5_part2.md); the effect on fresh seeds (-5.2%) is smaller than on development seeds (-8 to -10%).
- **C9 The rules survive when the alternative degrades too:** SUPPORTED: v4 and v5 stay below never-checking and CUSUM in all five scenarios; they rarely return to a tool after it recovers (S5), the reason for rule v6. *Limit:* EXPLORATORY: not pre-registered, 10 seeds, one setting, simulated tools. Confirmatory test planned with rule v6.
