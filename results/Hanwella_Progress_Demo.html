C2. Proof: a judge that repeats itself makes retry-checking backfire

If the judge gives the same wrong verdict again, retrying cannot fix a false alarm. A rule that assumes fresh verdicts underestimates the risk and pays for checks that cost more than never checking.

PROVEN

Orange area: success rates where a rule that assumes independent judge verdicts decides to check, yet never checking is cheaper. It grows as the judge repeats itself more (pi from 0.5 to 1).

Check against the simulator: Worst relative error 0.46% over 36 cases x 3 policies (80,000 simulated steps each); 14 s.
